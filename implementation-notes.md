# Implementation notes

## Week 1 — started 2026-10-26

Goal: a local Signal Room on the user's Mac. API, database, price sampler, scorer, two seed agents. No domain, no payments, no portal.

## Decisions

- SQLite by default, Postgres via `DATABASE_URL`. Same SQLAlchemy models either way. Removes the Docker dependency for a one-command local start.
- API and background jobs (price sampler, scorer, daily log) run in one process so `./start.sh` is the whole platform.
- Horizons: 15m, 1h, 4h, 24h. Pairs: BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP. Both chosen to match Kakalshi's native shape.
- Signed requests use EIP-191 personal_sign over `METHOD\nPATH\nTIMESTAMP\nNONCE\nSHA256(BODY)`. 60-second window, nonces stored and rejected on reuse.
- Signal bodies encrypted with Fernet under a per-agent key derived (HKDF) from the master key in `.env`.
- Daily log computes a Merkle root over the day's signal hashes. Writing it on-chain is Week 2.

## Deviations

- Kakalshi's shadow bets settle at a fixed market close, not exactly 15 minutes after the bet. The adapter posts a 15m-horizon signal at the moment the bet appears, so the Signal Room window and the Kalshi window differ by up to a few minutes. Acceptable for Week 1; revisit if scores diverge from Kakalshi's own record.
- Kakalshi shadow records carry `confidence: 0`. The adapter uses the price paid (market-implied probability of the chosen side), clamped to 0.50–0.95, as the signal confidence. Tunable in the adapter.

- Binance refuses US traffic (HTTP 451), confirmed on the first live run. Replaced with Bitstamp as the third feed. The spec said Binance; Bitstamp is US-reachable and keyless.
- 2026-10-27: Kakalshi adapter registers one Signal Room agent per Kakalshi strategy, keyed on the bet's `strategy` field. The original single `kakalshi-shadow` agent stays in the database with zero signals; harmless.
- 2026-10-27 (Week 2): Delisting reworked. Delisted agents may still post (the only road back) but cannot read the room, and their open signals are hidden from others. Reinstatement after 30 fresh resolved signals with BSS > 0. The trailing-90 delist window counts only signals resolved after the last delisting, so a reinstated agent starts clean; without that, the old misses re-delisted it on its next resolution (caught by the lifecycle test). Three delistings within 365 days retire the wallet. Database gained `agents.delisted_at`, added by a small in-code migration on startup.
- 2026-10-27 (Week 2): Daily Merkle root anchoring on Base Sepolia, gated on `ANCHOR_PRIVATE_KEY`. Payload is `signal-room:v1:<day>:<root>` in a zero-value self-transfer. Not yet exercised on-chain: needs a funded testnet wallet.
- 2026-10-27 (Week 2): Solana (Ed25519) wallets accepted alongside EVM for registration and signed requests. Client library takes `chain="solana"`.

- 2026-11-11, after the independent review of rule set 4 (see "Independent review of rule set 4" below), three more departures from the approved proposal, the first two approved by the user the same day ("Go"):
  - The luck odds and every range come from a betting account whose stakes are fixed when each call starts, over seven stake sizes (2.5% to 50%), not from "tries" averaged after the fact over five. The proposal's example 5 (two calls in one window, the stake "split $5 each", bankroll $96.25) becomes: $10 on the first, 10% of the $90 left on the second, bankroll $93.50. The proposal's promise that a record of plain calls keeps every number now holds for credits, skill, score, hit rate and delisting, not for luck odds and ranges. Decision 5 ("leave the stakes alone") is superseded: two smaller stakes were added because the account needs them.
  - A stated chance below what the call cost is scored as the cost.
  - A priced call whose price cannot be established is void after 30 minutes, not 72 hours; a result that is neither yes nor no is void at once; an answer from Kalshi that cannot be used refuses the call (503) instead of parking it.
- 2026-11-11, rule set 4, where the build differs from the approved proposal (each a clarification the proposal did not settle, none a change to the scoring rule):
  - A priced call may leave out its stated chance. It is then scored as stating exactly what it cost, which earns zero credits either way. The proposal had the control bot "claim nothing"; this is how. A stated chance, when given, must still be at least the limit.
  - A call that is not filled stays sealed until its market closes, like any open call, and only then shows as "unfilled". The proposal said it "stays on the record, visibly"; showing its side while the market was still open would have given a free read of the bot's signal.
  - A priced call's leaf includes the call's salt. Without it, anyone could try both sides against the public price and read the side of an open call from the day's published leaves.
  - A call on a market that does not exist or is not open is refused (422) rather than recorded. Under 60 seconds left is "not filled", as proposed.
  - When a call arrives, the room first settles that agent's previous call on the same coin if its market has closed, so the one-open-call rule never delays the next window's call.
  - The history fallback has no "contracts on offer" to check, so it requires at least 10 contracts traded in that minute instead.
  - The control bot bids up to 2c over the price it just read (never above 95c), so a tick between its look and the room's does not leave it unfilled. A fixed setting.
  - The proposal said the room would "store prices exactly as Kalshi gives them": they are stored as floating-point dollars parsed from Kalshi's decimal strings (0.927 for 92.7c), not as strings.

## Cold review, 2026-10-28

An independent review of the whole repo found 17 issues. Fixed in this batch:
- Critical: the signal hash committed only to public or guessable fields, so a reader could brute-force direction and confidence from the listing without spending a credit. Each signal now carries a random 128-bit salt, sealed with the body and revealed on resolution. Existing open signals keep their old hash; new ones use the salted one.
- Credit check-then-debit raced under concurrent reads (11 reads on 10 credits all succeeded). Serialized under a process lock.
- One-open-signal rule was check-then-insert only. Now a partial unique index in the database, added by migration; the race path returns 409.
- A single bad signal row (undecryptable body, zero price) stalled all resolution, daily logs, and nonce purging. Resolution is per-signal; a bad row is voided and logged.
- Resolution could pick a pre-horizon price sample. It now waits (inside tolerance) for a sample at or after the horizon. `resolved_at` is the horizon time, so windows are horizon-based and same-batch ordering is deterministic. `delisted_at` is the horizon of the tipping signal.
- Anchored day's stored root was overwritten every minute. Frozen once anchored; `/log/{day}` shows live and anchored roots side by side.
- Anchor tx fees were malformed for Base L2 (priority tip above max fee); now derived from the base fee. Waits for the receipt before recording; uses the pending nonce.
- Merkle tree duplicated the odd leaf (CVE-2012-2459 shape). Now RFC 6962 shape with leaf/node domain separation. Roots for 10-25..10-28 change; none were anchored.
- Signed message now covers the query string.
- Rate limit runs before signature verification and under a lock; a stolen API key alone cannot burn CPU.
- Interim registration throttle (20 wallets/hour) until membership gates it.
- Momentum control posted at exactly +3600s and hit 409 most hours; now posts at 2.5 min past each hour and uses the last completed candle.
- Kakalshi adapter retries transient errors (503/429/401) instead of dropping the bet, skips bets older than their horizon, and reads/writes its state file safely.
- Duplicate reference-price rows for the same minute are skipped.
- Dead import removed.

  - Copy rule. After reading another agent's open call on a coin, no posting on that coin (any window) until it closes. Hypothesis: a paid read could be reposted to build a record on someone else's work; nothing stopped it. Residual: a reader passing calls to a second wallet it controls is not detectable; reads cost credits, so it has to be earned first.

Each batch counts equally regardless of size; this is the conservative choice when coins are partly but not fully correlated.

- 2026-11-05, approved by the user (scoring change, rule set 3): a call's scored window starts at the first reference sample taken AFTER the post and runs one horizon from there. Hypothesis (confirmed by the reviewer's simulation with the room's own scorer): the old start price was the latest sample before the post, up to 60 s old (180 s worst case), so a bot watching live spot could post the direction of the move since that sample and be right without information: 60% at 59 s staleness, 69% gated on big moves, Proven within ~210 scored calls. Kakalshi CFM and Forecaster v2 both act on the first minute of a window and were benefiting without meaning to. Implementation: `price_at_submit` and `start_at` are filled on the next tick (a sample stamped at or after the post was fetched after it); `resolve_at` becomes start + horizon; the receipt hash no longer carries a price (hash format 3; the verify tool knows all three formats); the day's Merkle leaf is hash for a call without a start and sha256(hash, start_at, price_at_submit) once it has one, and a day is only anchored once every call posted that day has its start, so a rewritten start price breaks link 2. A post still needs a live sampler (503 otherwise); a call that gets no sample for 72 h (candle backfill included) is voided. Calls posted under the old rule keep their start and resolve under version 2. Tests in `tests/test_start_price.py`. - 2026-11-05, approved by the user (scoring change, record-level): tries are calls that share market time. A call made while the first call of the current try is still open joins that try; the next try opens once that first call has closed. Hypothesis (reviewer's simulation): 60-second batching let a bot spacing four correlated coins 61 s apart earn four "independent" tries from one market move; 35% of no-information bots reached "probably not luck" that way vs 3.5% posting together. Residual: a late member of a try can still overlap the next try's first call (at most the tail of one window), so adjacent tries are not perfectly independent; a transitive rule (any overlap merges) was rejected because continuously overlapping calls across coins would chain into one try forever. Hand-computed tests in `tests/test_batching.py`. Applies to all history (records are recomputed, stored scores untouched). Calibration bands now face the slice bar too (alpha/slices).

## Rule set 4: every call is judged against its bar, 2026-11-11

Approved by the user on 2026-11-11 ("move forward with your recommendations from the plan"), on the written proposal "Signal Room — Proposal: score every call against its price (rule set 4)", all six decisions as recommended, after a read-only check that Kalshi's prices, results, minute history and series fee settings are public without keys.

- Scoring change. Hypothesis: a contract bought for cost c pays 1 if it wins, so a buyer with no skill wins about c of the time and breaks even; winning clearly more often than cost (fees in) is evidence luck does not explain, on any venue, because the bar is the venue's own price. Every call has a bar r: 0.5 for a plain call, ask + fee for a priced one. Luck test: unit stake on a call returns (y - r)/r; a try's payoff is the mean over its calls; bankroll and thresholds as before. Fade side at r' = 1 - [(1 - bid) + fee(1 - bid)]. Credit = 4[(r - y)^2 - (p - y)^2]; skill = mean credit, computed as (mean ref Brier - mean Brier)/0.25, which is bit-identical to 1 - mean(BS)/0.25 at r = 0.5; delisting on the same. Edge range: shifts d the test does not reject with bars r + d (lower) and r' + d (upper). Skill range for a record holding priced calls: credits rescaled from [-4, 4] to [0, 1].
- Proof that plain calls are untouched: `tests/frozen/scoring_rule_set_3.py` is the scoring file as it stood at a475b9f; `tests/test_rule_set_4.py` checks every field of `record()` for 40 random plain records, plus `luck_odds`, `tries_to_reach`, `skill_score`, `should_delist`, `credit_for`, with `==`. The ten worked examples from the proposal are tested to the cent. Simulations kept as tests: a caller whose bar is exactly its true chance, checked after every call, is labelled under 5% of the time at 30-90c, 85-95c and 5-15c; paying a real spread and fee, under 5% on both sides.
- Numbers from the checks before approval (scratch runs, 2026-11-11): no-edge callers labelled 3-5% (bar = truth) and about 1.5% (real spread and fee); a caller winning 74% at a 70c cost needed a median of about 1,100 bets for "probably not luck" on a board of four, 80% at 75c about 530; a plain 55% caller about 1,000 tries. Unofficial preview on Kakalshi's 220 paper first-minute bets (its own recorded prices, one contract each, fee added): 74.5% wins against a 68.3c average cost, 130 tries, luck odds about even.
- Kalshi reader (`signal_room/kalshi.py`): read-only, no keys. One `GET /markets/{ticker}` gives status, times, both sides' bid and ask and the size at the YES bid and ask (the NO ask's size is the YES bid's). Fee: ceil(7 x p x (1 - p)) cents for one contract (Kalshi fee schedule effective 2026-07-07: "round up(0.07 x C x P x (1-P))", "There is no settlement fee"). `check_fee_rule` reads the series and refuses to fill unless it is still `quadratic` x 1. Anything missing or not understood raises `VenueError`; nothing is guessed. Tests run against the real answers saved from Kalshi on 2026-11-11 (`tests/fixtures/kalshi/`).
- Priced calls: `POST /signals/priced`, hash format 4. Fill decided from the price alone: ask <= limit, size >= 10, 0.05 <= ask <= 0.95, >= 60 s left. History fallback at the minute's highest ask. Result from the venue once `finalized`; void after 72 h. `scoring_version` 4 on every call resolved from now on (plain calls score exactly as under 3).
- Verify: a fourth check re-reads every scored priced call's result and that minute's price range from Kalshi directly. Tested against a room that lies consistently (hashes, leaf and anchor all agree; only Kalshi's record shows the price was never there).
- Seed agents: `favourite-control` (buys the dearer side of each coin's 15-minute market 90 s in, states no chance); the Kakalshi bridge posts priced calls as `kakalshi-<strategy>-priced` (limit = price paid, stated chance = limit + 0.05, file checked once a second, bets first seen over 30 s late not posted). `kakalshi-crypto-first-minute` receives nothing new; its record stands.

Known limits as first written (the first two were overtaken the same day by the review below: the stakes now start at 2.5% and tries no longer enter the odds): the smallest stake is 0.1, so an edge is only reliably certified above about 2.5 points at an even bar and about 1.5 points at a 70c bar. Tries can overlap at their edges under the batching rule; unchanged. The room checks that a price was on offer for 10 contracts; it does not check that anyone traded. The historical-candles address for markets Kalshi has archived is implemented from its documentation and not yet exercised against a real archived market.

## Independent review of rule set 4, 2026-11-11

Two reviewers who had not seen the build (one on the statistics, one on secrecy, failure handling and the database). 265 tests passed going in. What they found, and what was done. Their repro scripts were re-run against the fixes.

Scoring (the user approved the first two the same day; the rest are repairs to new code or to bugs):

- Critical, and in the live rules since 2026-10-31, not new: a try's payoff was the mean over however many calls ended up in it, and the number of calls can depend on how the first one is going. A bot with no edge makes one call and, only when it is losing, adds calls to the same window; a lost try then scores about -0.25, a won one +1. Confirmed here: 100% of such coin-flippers labelled "probably not luck" in 200 windows, priced or plain. Fix: `scoring.Timeline`, a real betting account. Each call's stake is a fixed fraction of the cash free when the call starts, set aside then, paid back with winnings at the close; a flat's stake comes back; only cash in hand counts (so open calls are passed in as `pending`). Hypothesis: every stake is fixed before anything about its call is known, so with no edge over the bars cash plus the fair value of open stakes is a supermartingale, cash alone is never more, and Ville's inequality holds at every instant, for any overlap, correlation or timing. Hand-computed tests in `tests/test_account.py`; the padding bot is now labelled 3-4% of the time (limit 5%), looked at after every close.
- The same fix closes the second finding (tries overlapping at their edges: 16.6% labelled under the old rule).
- Two smaller stakes (2.5%, 5%) were added. Without them an honest bot with four calls open at once over-bets (four stakes of 10% put 34% at work) and was certified in only about half the runs; with them it is certified as fast as before or faster (median 550 windows against 660 at 56% right, board of 4).
- Effect on the live record, computed from a copy of the database on 2026-11-11: no bot's verdict changes. Luck odds alone move (momentum's worse-odds 0.29 to 0.77, mean-reversion's 0.64 to 1.00, Kakalshi's 1.00 to 0.67).
- A stated chance under the cost earned a no-edge bot a trickle of credits (expected credit 4(p - r)(2π - p - r) is positive for p between 2π - r and r). Now scored as the cost.
- `edge_range` used one additive shift for every bar, which is confined to (-min bar, 1 - max fade bar) whatever the data, and left the observed edge outside its own range in about half of mixed-bar records. Now every bar is moved the same share of the way toward 0 or 1, both ends are tested against the cost bar, and the range is widened to hold the edge seen.
- Calibration recovered the stated chance from the Brier score and landed a stated 0.10 or 0.20 a hair under the band edge for wins only. Now rounded to the six places a Brier is stored at.
- Reinstatement tested skill > 0 on calls that claim nothing, whose skill is zero to within rounding (±1e-16): 12 of 20 delisted bots came back with zero credits. Now the reference is rounded as the stored Brier is, and reinstatement needs skill above 1e-9.
- Left as they are, written down: with prices both the "not luck" and the "worse" accounts can grow at once; the record is labelled worse than chance. The 30- and 90-day records restart the account at a moving edge, so the 1-in-20 bound is approximate there (5-7% at 90 days, up to 18% at 30 days for a slow bot); the all-time record is exact. The skill range switches test when a record gains its first priced call (still valid, a visible jump). At about 0.17 s per 1,000 calls `record()` will need caching by the time a bot has tens of thousands of calls.
