# Implementation notes

## Week 1 — started 2026-10-26

Goal: a local Signal Room on the user's Mac. API, database, price sampler, scorer, two seed agents. No domain, no payments, no portal.

## Decisions

- SQLite by default, Postgres via `DATABASE_URL`. Same SQLAlchemy models either way. Removes the Docker dependency for a one-command local start.
- API and background jobs (price sampler, scorer, daily log) run in one process so `./start.sh` is the whole platform.
- Horizons: 15m, 1h, 4h, 24h. Pairs: BTC, ETH, SOL, XRP. Both chosen to match Kakalshi's native shape.
- Signed requests use EIP-191 personal_sign over `METHOD\nPATH\nTIMESTAMP\nNONCE\nSHA256(BODY)`. 60-second window, nonces stored and rejected on reuse.
- Signal bodies encrypted with Fernet under a per-agent key derived (HKDF) from the master key in `.env`.
- Daily log computes a Merkle root over the day's signal hashes. Writing it on-chain is Week 2.

## Deviations

- Kakalshi's shadow bets settle at a fixed market close, not exactly 15 minutes after the bet. The adapter posts a 15m-horizon signal at the moment the bet appears, so the Signal Room window and the Kalshi window differ by up to a few minutes. Acceptable for Week 1; revisit if scores diverge from Kakalshi's own record.
- Kakalshi shadow records carry `confidence: 0`. The adapter uses the price paid (market-implied probability of the chosen side), clamped to 0.50–0.95, as the signal confidence. Tunable in the adapter.

- Binance refuses US traffic (HTTP 451), confirmed on the first live run. Replaced with Bitstamp as the third feed. The spec said Binance; Bitstamp is US-reachable and keyless.
- 2026-10-27: Kakalshi adapter registers one Signal Room agent per Kakalshi strategy, keyed on the bet's `strategy` field. The original single `kakalshi-shadow` agent stays in the database with zero signals; harmless.
- 2026-10-27 (Week 2): Delisting reworked. Delisted agents may still post (the only road back) but cannot read the room, and their open signals are hidden from others. Reinstatement after 30 fresh resolved signals with BSS > 0. The trailing-90 delist window counts only signals resolved after the last delisting, so a reinstated agent starts clean; without that, the old misses re-delisted it on its next resolution (caught by the lifecycle test). Three delistings within 365 days retire the wallet. Database gained `agents.delisted_at`, added by a small in-code migration on startup.
- 2026-10-27 (Week 2): Daily Merkle root anchoring on Base Sepolia, gated on `ANCHOR_PRIVATE_KEY`. Payload is `signal-room:v1:<day>:<root>` in a zero-value self-transfer. Not yet exercised on-chain: needs a funded testnet wallet.
- 2026-10-27 (Week 2): Solana (Ed25519) wallets accepted alongside EVM for registration and signed requests. Client library takes `chain="solana"`.

## Cold review, 2026-10-28

An independent review of the whole repo found 17 issues. Fixed in this batch:
- Critical: the signal hash committed only to public or guessable fields, so a reader could brute-force direction and confidence from the listing without spending a credit. Each signal now carries a random 128-bit salt, sealed with the body and revealed on resolution. Existing open signals keep their old hash; new ones use the salted one.
- Credit check-then-debit raced under concurrent reads (11 reads on 10 credits all succeeded). Serialized under a process lock.
- One-open-signal rule was check-then-insert only. Now a partial unique index in the database, added by migration; the race path returns 409.
- A single bad signal row (undecryptable body, zero price) stalled all resolution, daily logs, and nonce purging. Resolution is per-signal; a bad row is voided and logged.
- Resolution could pick a pre-horizon price sample. It now waits (inside tolerance) for a sample at or after the horizon. `resolved_at` is the horizon time, so windows are horizon-based and same-batch ordering is deterministic. `delisted_at` is the horizon of the tipping signal.
- Anchored day's stored root was overwritten every minute. Frozen once anchored; `/log/{day}` shows live and anchored roots side by side.
- Anchor tx fees were malformed for Base L2 (priority tip above max fee); now derived from the base fee. Waits for the receipt before recording; uses the pending nonce.
- Merkle tree duplicated the odd leaf (CVE-2012-2459 shape). Now RFC 6962 shape with leaf/node domain separation. Roots for 10-25..10-28 change; none were anchored.
- Signed message now covers the query string.
- Rate limit runs before signature verification and under a lock; a stolen API key alone cannot burn CPU.
- Interim registration throttle (20 wallets/hour) until membership gates it.
- Momentum control posted at exactly +3600s and hit 409 most hours; now posts at 2.5 min past each hour and uses the last completed candle.
- Kakalshi adapter retries transient errors (503/429/401) instead of dropping the bet, skips bets older than their horizon, and reads/writes its state file safely.
- Duplicate reference-price rows for the same minute are skipped.
- Dead import removed.

Deferred, with reasons:
- Resolved signals keep direction/confidence in plain columns. They are public after resolution by design; noted, not changed.
- The signed-request dependency is async and does blocking work on the event loop. Fine at this scale; revisit before public launch.
- Two scoring-rule changes, approved by the user 2026-10-28 and shipped: (1) flat outcomes are recorded and counted but not scored (no Brier, no credit, not in n); previously a 0.50 call on a flat scored as perfect. (2) Credits are signed, `4 * (0.25 - BS)` with no floor, so a wrong call costs credits; previously random guessing at high confidence earned ~0.5/signal. Existing resolved rows keep their old brier/credit values; the change applies from this commit forward.
- 2026-10-29: Every record now carries its own uncertainty: `p_luck` (exact one-sided binomial: chance a coin-flipper matches the hit rate), 95% Wilson interval on hit rate, 95% interval on BSS from the standard error of mean Brier, and an `evidence` label (insufficient / none / suggestive / strong). Scoring rules are versioned (`scoring.SCORING_VERSION`); each resolved signal stores the version it was scored under, and a rule change never rescores history. Migration backfills version 1/2 on existing rows from their shape. Test proves a rule change cannot alter an already-resolved signal.
- 2026-10-29 (evening): Sleep resilience: a signal whose horizon fell in a sampler gap is scored from the exchanges' own 1-minute candles (median of those that answer, rows labelled `backfill`) instead of voided; it only voids if no candle can be had for 72 hours. Tick gaps are logged. Discovery: `/llms.txt`, `/.well-known/agent.json`, tagged OpenAPI, and `agents/mcp_server.py` wrapping the client as MCP tools (mcp 2.x SDK). Third seed agent `meanrev`, the mirror of momentum, to bracket the scorer from both sides.

- 2026-10-31, approved by the user (scoring change): calls posted within 60 seconds of a batch's first call count as one try in `p_luck`, `evidence` and both ranges. Hypothesis: same-moment calls on correlated coins are not independent, so the binomial overstated certainty. Seen live: mean-reversion read "probably not luck" (1 in 68) on 112 calls that were really 34 hourly batches during one choppy weekend; momentum, its mirror, read the opposite. Method: exact sign-flip test over batches (each batch's right-minus-half flips sign under "no information"), identical to the binomial when every batch has one call; ranges widened by the cluster design effect, floored at 1. Hand-computed tests in `tests/test_batching.py`. On the live data: mean-reversion 1 in 68 → 1 in 17 (now "could easily be luck"); CFM 1 in 16 → 1 in 7. Hit rate, skill and leaderboard score are unchanged by this. Records now report `tries`.
- 2026-10-31, decided by Claude on the user's delegation (scoring change): the 15 flats scored under rule set 1 are left out of every aggregate; only right/wrong calls are scored. Stored rows are untouched (history is not rewritten), and the change can only lower a record. On the live data: momentum skill +0.01 → -0.04, CFM +0.09 → +0.08. Credits those flats minted stay in the ledger as what happened.
- 2026-10-31 (evening), approved by the user ("start in whichever order works best") after he asked for the math to be tightened, not softened:
  - Copy rule. After reading another agent's open call on a coin, no posting on that coin (any window) until it closes. Hypothesis: a paid read could be reposted to build a record on someone else's work; nothing stopped it. Residual: a reader passing calls to a second wallet it controls is not detectable; reads cost credits, so it has to be earned first.
  - Luck odds valid under constant checking. Hypothesis: the room recomputes every minute, so a one-look test's 1-in-20 promise does not hold. Replaced by a betting test (test martingale): 1 / mean over stakes {0.1..0.5} of the product of (1 + stake * x) over batches, x = (right - wrong) / size. Ville's inequality bounds false verdicts at any stopping time. Simulation (300 coin-flippers, 300 calls, checked every call): one-look test fooled 22%, betting test 4%. Cost: a true 60% caller needs ~196 separate tries for "probably not luck" (was ~71) and ~406 for "proven" (was ~241). Current bankroll, not running max, so verdicts fade with records. Each batch counts equally regardless of size; this is the conservative choice when coins are partly but not fully correlated.
  - Verdict ladder replaces the evidence label: unproven / luck / probably_not_luck / proven / worse_than_chance; delisted and retired override on public records. Skill shown as points (BSS x 100). Progress projection replays the record so far.
  - Slices (one coin or window) multiply luck odds by the number of slices of that kind (Bonferroni) and are marked exploratory. Status shows per-coin splits as exploratory only.
  - Calibration bands (50-60 ... 90-100%) with batch-aware Wilson ranges; stated confidence recovered exactly from each call's Brier. Always-long baseline beside the 1:1 return.
  - Read price by verdict (1 / 2 / 4), reader-control agent.
  - Finding on live data: CFM's 60% hit rate is carried by multi-coin batches that went all one way (three 4-for-4, three 3-for-3); single bets were 10 right, 14 wrong, and batches 22 mostly right vs 19 mostly wrong. Counted as separate tries it is a coin flip so far, and the projection says it is not heading toward a verdict.
  - Deferred: the hit-rate and skill ranges are still one-look ranges (only the verdict and luck odds are constant-checking safe); a records-wide correction for many agents competing on one board; verdicts over sliding windows.

- 2026-11-04, approved by the user ("yes, build the tighter ranges as long as the math checks out") (scoring change, display only): every range on a record (right rate, skill, calibration bands) is now a confidence sequence from the betting test, replacing the Wilson and standard-error intervals with a design-effect widening. Hypothesis: the ranges are recomputed every minute and watched continuously, so a one-look 95% range misses the truth far more than 5% of the time. Method: one value per try (batch mean); a mean m is ruled out once either side's bankroll (mean over the fixed stakes of the product of 1 + (c/m)(v - m), or the mirror with c/(1 - m)) reaches 2/alpha = 40; each bankroll is monotone in m, so each edge is found by bisection. Simulation (1,000 callers, 300 calls, checked after every call; true rates 50/55/60%): one-look ranges missed the truth 29.5/30.3/32.0% of the time, the new ranges 3.9/4.3/4.2%. On the live data: CFM 46-70% -> 34-69%, mean-reversion 47-60% -> 44-64%, momentum 40-52% -> 36-53%; CFM's 70-80% calibration band moves from overconfident to ok (no longer enough evidence). Verdicts, rankings, credits, read prices and stored scores unchanged. Design effect, Wilson and standard-error interval code removed.

## Second outside-eyes review, 2026-11-04 (night)

Two independent reviewers (security/ops; statistics/bots) at e98a62f: 16 findings. Steven approved the three scoring items and the seven non-scoring "should fix" items; the six minor items are left as is (registration throttle lockout, the unused `anchor.verify` was removed anyway, `/me` on a corrupt row, the daily report's "verdict moved" for delisted bots, status's all-time slices under a 90-day headline, "Sharpe" being a t-statistic).

- 2026-11-05, approved by the user (scoring change, rule set 3): a call's scored window starts at the first reference sample taken AFTER the post and runs one horizon from there. Hypothesis (confirmed by the reviewer's simulation with the room's own scorer): the old start price was the latest sample before the post, up to 60 s old (180 s worst case), so a bot watching live spot could post the direction of the move since that sample and be right without information: 60% at 59 s staleness, 69% gated on big moves, Proven within ~210 scored calls. Kakalshi CFM and Forecaster v2 both act on the first minute of a window and were benefiting without meaning to. Implementation: `price_at_submit` and `start_at` are filled on the next tick (a sample stamped at or after the post was fetched after it); `resolve_at` becomes start + horizon; the receipt hash no longer carries a price (hash format 3; the verify tool knows all three formats); the day's Merkle leaf is hash for a call without a start and sha256(hash, start_at, price_at_submit) once it has one, and a day is only anchored once every call posted that day has its start, so a rewritten start price breaks link 2. A post still needs a live sampler (503 otherwise); a call that gets no sample for 72 h (candle backfill included) is voided. Calls posted under the old rule keep their start and resolve under version 2. Tests in `tests/test_start_price.py`. Side effects: the agents' post logs no longer show a price; Forecaster v2's history check now scores from the minute after the post (W+2m to W+17m), the stretch the room scores, so live and history match; v1's hourly replay is one minute early (hourly candles), noted in its docstring.
- 2026-11-05, approved by the user (scoring change, record-level): tries are calls that share market time. A call made while the first call of the current try is still open joins that try; the next try opens once that first call has closed. Hypothesis (reviewer's simulation): 60-second batching let a bot spacing four correlated coins 61 s apart earn four "independent" tries from one market move; 35% of no-information bots reached "probably not luck" that way vs 3.5% posting together. Residual: a late member of a try can still overlap the next try's first call (at most the tail of one window), so adjacent tries are not perfectly independent; a transitive rule (any overlap merges) was rejected because continuously overlapping calls across coins would chain into one try forever. Hand-computed tests in `tests/test_batching.py`. Applies to all history (records are recomputed, stored scores untouched). Calibration bands now face the slice bar too (alpha/slices).
- 2026-11-05, non-scoring fixes from the review: (1) verify checks the anchor's sender against the room's published wallet, reports when the anchor was written, flags an anchor more than 48 h after the day as late, words VERIFIED as "nothing has changed since it was anchored", skips open calls without reading them (no credit spend, no 402 crash; NOT YET until the day fully closes); the listing carries `resolved` for that. (2) A delisted author's open calls are 404 by id, not just hidden from the listing. (3) Anchoring runs in its own thread beside the tick; a sent transaction is stored as `pending_tx` and polled on later ticks, never resent, so a slow chain cannot stall price sampling or queue duplicate transactions (tested with a fake chain). (4) Price feeds: a feed whose own timestamp is over 120 s old is dropped (Kraken gives no timestamp, so it cannot be checked), a feed over 1% from the median is dropped, two feeds that disagree by over 1% are both dropped, and a minute written from fewer than three feeds is logged `price_degraded` (`feed_stale`, `feed_outlier` events). (5) The three control bots and both forecasters survive any error in a cycle (network blip, server restart) instead of dying silently.
- 2026-11-05, the six minor review items, on the user's go: the registration cap now counts completed registrations (a stranger hammering the unsigned challenge step cannot lock everyone out) and challenges are capped per source address (30/hour); `/log` reports an anchored root only for anchored days and the dead `anchor.verify` is gone; a sealed call that cannot be opened shows null direction and confidence instead of breaking `/me` and `/room/{id}`; the daily report applies the delisted/retired override to "before" as well as "now"; status's per-coin and per-window slices use the same 90-day window as the headline; `sharpe` is now per call (mean net return over its spread), not multiplied by sqrt(n). Note: `test_rate_limit_returns_429_before_signature_work` can flake when a run crosses a minute boundary; it predates tonight and passes on rerun.
